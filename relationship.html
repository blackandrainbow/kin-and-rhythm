The relationship

What the app is to her

Most wellness apps behave like a coach with a clipboard, or like a slot machine. This one is meant to sit closer to an almanac on the kitchen shelf and a friend who remembers.

That choice shows up in what it does, what it refuses to do, how it talks, and how the relationship changes over time.

Its roles

Four things it is, and one it is not

  • A mirror

    It shows her own record back to her, in her own words and pictures. It never turns that record into a grade.

  • A keeper

    It holds the things worth keeping: glimmers, moon pages, the rhythms that became hers.

  • A pause

    It gives the hard minute somewhere to go, and trusts her with the decision afterward.

  • A go-between

    It helps her ask one real person to hold her word, then steps out of the way.

  • Not a coach, a judge or a feed

    It does not set targets for her, rank her days, or show her anyone else's.

Its promises

What it will always do, and never do

It will

  • Ask one question a day, then say she is done
  • Shrink the plan when she has less to give
  • Treat a missed day as nothing at all
  • Offer every choice in a hard moment, including the comfortable one
  • Hand her own data back in plain words
  • Let her change her path, her rituals and her mind

It never will

  • Count streaks, award points or hand out badges
  • Show a goal weight, or any weight on her main screen
  • Tell her she is behind, off track or starting over
  • Show a feed, likes or anyone else's progress
  • Use guilt to bring her back
  • Claim to diagnose, treat or cure anything

Over time

A relationship designed to loosen

Success is not measured by time in the app. It is measured by how little she comes to need it.

  1. Week one

    Getting to know her

    Time in the app

    Daily. She finds her pattern, sets her first rhythms and learns where Pause is.

  2. First moons

    The ritual takes over

    Time in the app

    Ten seconds most days, and five real minutes at each new and full moon.

  3. A season in

    Things she can hold

    Time in the app

    Rhythms retire. The moon card and the deck do the reminding, and her witness does the asking.

  4. Later

    An almanac on the shelf

    Time in the app

    She opens it when a season turns, or to look back through the grid. That is the goal.

Who holds what

Three keepers

  • The app holdsthe record

    Her days, her moons, her glimmers.

  • She holdsthe practice

    The rhythms happen in her kitchen, not on a screen.

  • Her kin holdsthe word

    One friend, one question, at the full moon.

Trust

What she can rely on

Her data is hers. In this prototype everything she enters stays on her own device.

The optional things are really optional. Weight and the card ritual are off unless she turns them on, and she can turn them off again.

The claims are modest. The app talks about paths wearing in and energy returning. It makes no medical promises.

She can leave and come back. Nothing resets, nothing expires, and her second-nature rhythms stay hers.
