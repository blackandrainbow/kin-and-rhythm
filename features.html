First run

Finding her pattern

Onboarding walks through six ordinary moments: the 5:40pm crunch, twenty minutes alone, how a hard week ends. Then it asks why she wants this change, and she can say it out loud. Her answers point to two or three stress patterns, and she chooses the one that sounds like her right now. Until she chooses, the app is deliberately gray. Then it blooms into her color.

Why it is thereA quiz that hands down a verdict feels like a label. Letting her pick from the closest matches keeps the choice hers, and the pattern is always described as a season she is in.

  • Five patterns: Sun, Wind, Hearth, Tide, Frost
  • Each comes with its own path, color and first two rhythms
  • She can change path later without starting over

Soon after

The cup audit

Before anything is asked of her plate, the app asks what restores her. Five outlets: body and energy, kin and community, nervous system, play and expression, curiosity and spirit. For each she can tap what fits, search, or say it in her own words and have it written down. At the end it reads her cup back to her exactly as she described it.

Why it is thereA depleted mother is not short of food rules. If fewer than two outlets have anything in them, the app says so plainly, starts her path there, and lets food wait.

  • She can talk, and it is written down for her
  • Empty outlets are named, not scored
  • Her path and her spark rhythm are built from what she says

Every day

The ten-second touch

Today asks one question: how much do you have? Her answer sizes the day. On a low day, or in her inner autumn or winter, each rhythm shrinks to its smallest version. Once she has answered, the app tells her she is done and that the rest happens out there.

Why it is thereThe daily visit is meant to be short. An app that ends its own session earns more trust than one that tries to keep her scrolling.

  • Rhythms are tied to something she already does, like pouring her first cup
  • Nothing keeps score. A missed day changes nothing
  • Second-nature rhythms drop off the list

Any time

Pause

The round button in the middle is always there. She says what is happening, out loud or typed, or taps the closest of five moments. The app shows her own words back to her and asks what the version of her who cares about her own reasons would do next. A breath or body reset is there to try first, and is never required.

Why it is thereThis is the moment most plans fail. Giving the hard minute somewhere to go, with every option allowed, is what makes a better choice possible.

  • Talking is written down for her, so there is nothing to type
  • "Have it, sitting down, and enjoy it" is always one of the choices
  • Her words are kept in her Almanac, and the app learns her hardest hour

The plan

Her path and its rhythms

Each pattern walks the same four phases (Rest, Rebuild, Rise, Release) with different rhythms, two at a time. A rhythm is one small steady thing. Marking one opens a ten-second pause to feel it, then asks how much effort it took. Each rhythm shows a trail that wears in with every rep and never resets.

Why it is thereHabits stick through repetition in the same context, and through feeling the result. Effort falling over time is the honest sign that something has become hers.

  • Faint track, footpath, clear path, well-worn path
  • When a rhythm feels easy three times running, it can become second nature
  • Food guidance adds to what she already eats. Nothing is taken away

The plan

Her table

A canvas of go-to meals: four boxes each for breakfast, lunch, dinner and the three o'clock bridge. A new mother starts with the boxes fairly blank and fills them over time. Every box stays visible, so she can see what she is building.

Why it is thereDeciding what to eat, three times a day, is where tired mothers lose the most energy. A small rotation she trusts removes the deciding.

  • One each for breakfast, lunch and dinner counts as a full rotation
  • Suggested boxes are marked, and never overwrite the ones she chose
  • It sits inside Path, beside her rhythms and practices

The plan

Filling a box

Tapping an open box asks what she usually has for that meal, once. Then it offers two routes. First, adjust what she already eats: keep the coffee, add protein. Second, discover something new, filtered by time, pantry budget, whether the kids will eat it, and what she leaves out. She can also add a meal her family already loves.

Why it is thereStarting from what she already eats respects her life as it is. Nothing is taken away, and the first change is the smallest one available.

  • Ideas from her own food heritage come first
  • Warm food is ranked higher in her inner autumn and winter
  • On a day she has not much, it opens on zero-cook ideas

Her record

The Almanac

A wheel of her four inner seasons, following her cycle or, when she has none, the real moon. Each day she checked in becomes a light on the ring, bigger when she had more to give. Underneath, the app hands back what it has noticed: where her energy lives, which rhythm goes with a good day, and when she needs the most care.

Why it is thereShe gives the app a little every day. The Almanac is where it gives something back that she could not have seen on her own.

  • Real moon phase, first light, golden hour and sunset
  • Insights only appear once there is enough data to mean something
  • Her gentle season is flagged before it arrives

Twice a moon

The setting and the gathering

At the new moon she sets the next two weeks: a wish, the obstacle that usually gets in the way, what she will do when it does, how much she has, and one word. At the full moon she gathers: what she did, how each rhythm held, what she noticed, and whether to stay in this phase or move on.

Why it is thereThis is the real ritual. Two weeks is long enough to see something and short enough to adjust, and the moon gives it a date nobody has to invent.

  • "Not much" for the two weeks cuts the plan to one rhythm
  • Phase changes are decided here, not by a streak
  • Each setting ends with a moon card to print and keep

With someone

Kin

At the new moon she can name one witness. The app writes the message for her: her word, what the moon is for, and one request, which is to ask how it went at the full moon. The witness needs no app. She can also keep the words of friends she is holding, and is reminded to ask them.

Why it is thereWith no streaks, something else has to hold her to it. One person who knows is lighter than a group and stronger than a notification.

  • One witness, not a feed
  • Her name is carried on the moon card
  • "Everything feels too heavy" in Pause becomes "Text Jess"

Noticing

The glimmer grid

Once a day she can keep one small beautiful thing: a photo, a line, or both. They gather into a quiet grid, each tile marked with the inner season it was kept in. There is no feed and nobody else sees it.

Why it is thereAttention goes where it is trained. Stopping to take in an ordinary moment is also what helps a new habit stick, so this does two jobs.

  • Photos are cropped square and kept on her device
  • The grid closes every full moon gathering
  • It can be printed as a contact sheet

Only if she wants

The card deck and the scale

Two layers are entirely optional. She can choose a tarot deck in onboarding; it is mailed to her, the app turns a card until it arrives, and after that she pulls her own and tells the app what she drew. And she can keep a private weight log, with no goal number, that never appears on her main screen.

Why it is thereA mother who finds tarot silly and a mother who loves it should both feel at home. The same goes for the scale.

  • Each card becomes a lens for mind, body and spirit
  • "Not tracking" is treated as a complete answer
  • Both can be switched on or off at any time
