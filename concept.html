The pattern

Every action has the same four parts

Nothing in the app is a free-floating task. Each thing she does is built the same way, which keeps the daily cost low and makes the record worth having.

  1. A cueIt starts from something already in her day, or from the moon. Never from a streak.After I pour my first cup
  2. One small actA tap wherever possible. Typing is always optional.She taps the rhythm, feels it for ten seconds, and says how much effort it took
  3. Something keptThe least the app needs: an answer, a rep, an hour.One rep, with the date and the effort
  4. Something backSomething now, and something later that she could not have seen for herself.Now, the trail wears in. Later, the Almanac shows which days it goes with

The lines in italics follow one action, keeping a rhythm, through all four parts.

Her plan

Built from her own answers

She never fills in a plan. The app asks about her life, and each answer turns into a working part.

She tells itIt becomes
In first run
What fills her cupHer spark rhythm, in her own words, with her other answers as swaps. If she says nothing does right now, a small spark becomes one of her first two rhythms.
How six ordinary moments goA count for each of the five patterns. The closest two or three are offered, and she picks.
The pattern she picksHer colour, her path through the four phases, her first two rhythms, and how food and movement are framed.
What she is here forThe wish offered first at each new moon, and the question Pause asks before she chooses.
Her hardest hourA nudge on Today during that hour, pointing at Pause. After three pauses, the Almanac shows the hour she actually reaches for it.
Her cycle, or noneHer inner season each day, by her cycle if she has one and by the moon if she does not. The season sizes her rhythms and ranks warm meals higher.

Whether to track weightA private log with no goal number, kept off her main screen. Not tracking is a complete answer.

How much she hasEach day it sizes her rhythms. Each new moon it sizes the fortnight, and "not much" cuts the plan to one rhythm.

A suggestion

What to settle first

Not everything above is equally urgent. This is the order that unblocks the most.

1. The safety rulesNothing goes in front of real mothers until the app knows what to do about disordered eating and low mood.
2. Today's first screenfulIt is the screen she sees every day, and the ten-second promise rests on it.
3. A reminder that fitsThe moon nights carry the accountability, and right now they depend on her remembering.
4. The words for week oneNine new words is a lot. Cut to the few she needs before her first full moon.
5. Five real mothersBefore anything gets polished, watch five people go through first run and one Pause.
